What counts as blue-chip

A share must pass all six tests. Failures are hidden unless you switch them on above.

  1. Index member: in one of the indexes on the tabs above the table.
  2. Size: market value of at least US$20 billion, with other currencies converted to US dollars.
  3. Financial strength: debt no more than 3.5× yearly cash earnings (EBITDA). Banks, brokers and asset managers are exempt, because the measure does not fit how they are financed.
  4. Profitable: positive net income over the last 12 months.
  5. Track record: trading for at least 10 years.
  6. China-free: not headquartered in China, Hong Kong or Macau, and not on the China-exposure list (about 15%+ of revenue from Greater China, or major operations there).

How the agent decides

Seven checks, one point each. Yes needs at least 6 points and a profitable company. Two things each cost a point: a fall of 40% or more in three months, because a drop that steep usually has a reason worth reading first, and sales down 5% or more on a year earlier, because a cheap share with shrinking sales is often cheap for good reason.

  1. Profitable over the last 12 months (required).
  2. Profit margin of 15% or more.
  3. Forward P/E of 20 or less.
  4. Forward P/E below trailing P/E (earnings expected to grow).
  5. Debt no more than 2× yearly cash earnings (EBITDA).
  6. Free cash flow yield of 3% or more.
  7. Analyst average target at least 25% above today's price, from at least 5 analysts.

Banks, brokers and asset managers do not report EBITDA or free cash flow, so checks 5 and 6 become return on equity of 12% or more and earnings yield of 7% or more (profit as a share of the price). The agent reads the numbers only. It does not read the news, so use Deep dive before acting.

About this data

  • Every weekday after the US market closes, every member of each index is re-screened and this page updates automatically. Pick an index with the tabs above the table; All shows every index together.
  • Prices are in each share's own currency: US dollars, pounds, euros, rupees or Australian dollars. Company figures such as market value and profit are converted to US dollars so they can be compared.
  • Figures for shares outside the US come from the same Yahoo feed but are checked less widely, so look twice at anything that seems off.
  • The index figure above the % 3M column is that index's own change over the same 3 months, so you can see whether a share fell with the market or on its own.
  • 1M, 3M, 1Y, 2Y are the closing prices on or just before those dates; % 3M compares today's close with the 3M price.
  • Prices and fundamentals come from Yahoo Finance. Index membership comes from Wikipedia's list for each index.
  • The China-exposure list is kept by hand from company annual reports and is approximate. Ask for names to be added or removed.
  • Company status: Growing when forward P/E is at least 10% under trailing, Slowing when 10% over, otherwise Level.
  • Target buy starts 5% under the current price; Target divest starts at the analyst average target.
  • Coverage: few blue-chips fall hard, so slider values past the deepest fall show nothing.
  • Gaps: if Yahoo has no market value or profit figure for a share, it is treated as failing the blue-chip test until the figure returns.
